The regulation

Five pillars. All of them operational requirements.

DORA covers five interconnected areas. A gap in any one of them is visible during supervisory examination. We work across all five, grounded in the Level 1 regulation and the Level 2 RTS and ITS that specify how compliance is demonstrated.

Pillar 01

ICT Risk Management Framework

Governance structure, risk appetite, policies, controls and evidence that the framework operates. Not a document that describes the right process but a system of records showing it runs.

Pillar 02

ICT Incident Classification, Reporting & Response

Classification criteria, escalation thresholds, the major incident reporting chain to competent authorities, and the internal records that support each notification.

Pillar 03

Digital Operational Resilience Testing

Threat-led penetration testing for significant institutions, vulnerability assessments for others, and the methodology, results and remediation records regulators require.

Pillar 04

ICT Third-Party Risk & Register of Information

Contractual requirements for ICT providers, the Register of Information in the format ESAs examine, concentration risk analysis and ongoing oversight procedures.

Pillar 05

Information Sharing Arrangements

Participation in threat intelligence sharing, documentation of sharing agreements and their scope, and integration with the incident response function.

Our work

What an engagement produces

Gap Assessment

We map your current posture against the five pillars, the RTS on ICT risk management, and the ITS on the Register of Information. The output is a prioritized gap register with specific corrective actions and the evidence each closure requires, not a summary of things you already knew.

Register of Information

The Register of Information is the document supervisors examine most closely. We build or review it against the ESA templates: contractual arrangements, ICT service categories, substitutability assessments, concentration analysis and the supporting data quality controls.

ICT Third-Party Risk Program

We design the oversight program: due diligence criteria, contractual minimum requirements, ongoing monitoring procedures and escalation paths for critical or important ICT providers. The program is built to hold up when a supervisory authority asks how concentration risk is managed.

Incident Classification & Reporting

We build the classification framework using the RTS criteria, draft the reporting templates for major incident notifications, and document the internal escalation chain so the reporting obligation can be met within regulatory timeframes.

Resilience Testing Scope & Methodology

We define the testing scope against DORA's requirements, review existing test programs for coverage gaps, and produce the methodology and results documentation the regulation requires.

Internal Audit of DORA Posture

We conduct an independent internal audit of the DORA compliance program, evaluate evidence quality across all five pillars and produce findings with specific closure criteria. The audit is conducted by credentialed lead auditors, not junior consultants.

Who DORA applies to

In-scope entities and ICT providers

DORA applies directly to financial entities regulated under EU law: credit institutions, payment institutions, e-money institutions, investment firms, insurance and reinsurance undertakings, crypto-asset service providers and others. It also applies to critical ICT third-party providers designated by the ESAs.

If you are a US-headquartered financial institution with EU operations, an ICT provider whose financial-entity clients are subject to DORA, or a financial group determining which entities fall within scope, the first task is a clear applicability analysis. We start there.
