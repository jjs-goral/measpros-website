The Compliance Anchor

One standard. Every jurisdiction covered.

AI regulation is not coming from one place. The EU AI Act, US federal procurement rules, New York Local Law 144, California SB 813, DORA each has its own requirements. ISO/IEC 42001 is the structural foundation that satisfies the governance layer across all of them.

EU AI Act

The Act's technical standards work explicitly references ISO/IEC 42001 as the presumptive conformity path for high-risk AI systems. An AIMS built to 42001 provides documented evidence across the Act's Article 9 risk management requirements.

US Government Contracting

Microsoft, Palantir, and major federal prime contractors now include ISO/IEC 42001 in their vendor qualification requirements. If you supply AI-enabled products or services to US government programs, this certification is becoming table stakes.

Global Enterprise Procurement

Procurement teams at financial institutions, healthcare systems, and defence contractors are adding 42001 to their third-party risk checklists. Certification answers the questionnaire before it's sent.

Bias & Algorithmic Audits

New York Local Law 144 and California's forthcoming bias audit requirements call for documented AI governance processes. An ISO/IEC 42001-certified AIMS provides the framework that makes those audits reproducible and defensible.

The Competitive Reality

This isn't a future requirement. It's a present one.

Organisations that move now build the governance infrastructure on their own timeline. Those that wait will rebuild it under regulatory pressure faster, more expensively, and with less control over the outcome.

Microsoft requires it

Microsoft's AI supplier framework now references ISO/IEC 42001 compliance. If your organisation sells into Microsoft's ecosystem, you need to be able to demonstrate this.

Government contracts are evolving

Federal and state agencies are adding AI governance criteria to procurement. Countries including the UK, Canada, and Singapore are building 42001 into their public sector AI standards.

Insurance is catching up

Cyber and E&O underwriters are beginning to ask for AI governance documentation. Certification provides the documented controls that risk carriers want to see.

The EU AI Act is live

Full enforcement of the EU AI Act began in 2025. Organisations operating in European markets need documented AI governance 42001 is the fastest path to that documentation.

Board-level scrutiny

AI risk is now an audit committee topic. Certification gives the board a recognised framework to point to the same function ISO 27001 plays for cybersecurity governance.

First-mover advantage

In sectors where certification is not yet universal, being certified now differentiates you in procurement, partnerships, and client conversations before it becomes a baseline requirement.
